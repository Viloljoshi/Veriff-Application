Begin with product outcomes.

Model metrics are useful only when they connect to fraud, conversion, operations and customer trust.

Precision and recall describe model behaviour.

False accepts, false rejects, conversion, review volume and latency describe its product consequences.

Once the issue is concentrated, the problem becomes much cheaper to reason about.

See how I’d query it (illustrative example for this case) + 
SELECT
  document_country,
  document_type,
  document_variant,
  platform,
  COUNT(*) AS sessions,
  AVG(first_pass_approved) AS first_pass_rate,
  AVG(resubmission_requested) AS resubmission_rate,
  AVG(review_required) AS review_rate,
  PERCENTILE_CONT(0.95)
    WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY decision_latency_ms) AS p95_latency
FROM verification_sessions
WHERE enterprise_customer_id = :customer
  AND model_version = :release
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3, 4
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 500
ORDER BY first_pass_rate ASC;

Simplified illustrative query, intended to show the analytical path rather than Veriff’s internal schema.

Working discipline

What changed?

Compare release, traffic, device and failure-reason distributions to baseline. Trace the pipeline before prescribing the fix.

Falsification test

What would make the domain-gap hypothesis wrong?

  • Frontend or capture regression
  • SDK release or traffic-mix shift
  • Policy configuration change
  • Annotation drift or pipeline issue
  • Operational routing change

If the evidence doesn’t support a data/model gap, don’t retrain another model.

My PM responsibility

I would not tell the ML team which loss function to use. I would make the business cost asymmetry explicit, define the target outcome and guardrails, and make sure we’re optimizing the right problem.

07 · Make the decision

What would I actually do?

Choose the narrowest intervention that protects the customer while the evidence becomes stronger.

A Lower the threshold globallyFast, but the wrong level Not first choice

Potential upside: may recover genuine-user conversion.

Risk: changes unaffected cohorts and may increase false acceptance.

B Route the affected cohort to HITLContain uncertainty now Temporary containment

Benefit: limits immediate customer damage and protects uncertain decisions.

Cost: latency, review expense and operational capacity.

C Roll back the model releaseWhen isolation is unsafe Conditional

Use when severity is material, the timing points clearly to the release, and the risk cannot be isolated safely.

D Close the domain gapIf root cause supports it Preferred durable path
Representative data→ Annotation + QA→ Dedicated slice→ Model / calibration→ Controlled rollout

The goal isn’t “retrain the model.”

It is to fix the customer outcome without creating unacceptable risk elsewhere.

09 · The release contract

How I’d know the change is actually better.

Do not celebrate an offline metric until the target cohort improves without exporting harm elsewhere.

  2. 02Slice-level evaluation
  3. 03Shadow
  4. 04Canary
  5. 05Partial rollout
  6. 06Full rollout
  7. 07Continuous monitoring

↩ Roll back at any stage if a guardrail fails.

A better offline model is not automatically a better product.

I would ship only when the target cohort improves without moving unacceptable fraud, conversion, latency or operational risk somewhere else.

10 · Enterprise communication

What I’d tell the customer.

Enterprise customer

“Our verification conversion has dropped since the latest model release. Is the new model causing this?”

My response

You’re right to flag this. We’ve confirmed the drop and narrowed it to one document cohort. The rest of your traffic is stable.

We’re routing that cohort to review while we test the release. I’ll return with the confirmed cause and the evidence required to roll out a fix.

Specific about what we know. Explicit about uncertainty. No promises before evidence.

Customer credibility comes from explaining what failed, who is affected, what is being done and how we’ll know it is fixed.

A second kind of decision

Sometimes the right answer is “not yet.”

An enterprise customer asks for immediate support for a new physical-document variant.

What must be true first
  • Representative evidence exists
  • Classification and extraction are validated
  • Fraud behaviour is understood
  • An evaluation slice is ready
  • Operational fallback exists
  • Data use and monitoring are approved

Customer-centric means knowing what evidence turns “not yet” into “yes.”
